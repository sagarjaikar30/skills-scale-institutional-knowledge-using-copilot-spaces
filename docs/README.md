# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This README provides a central entry point to the OctoAcme project management guidance and summarizes the standard project lifecycle used across the organization.

## Project Management Process Overview

OctoAcme follows a practical project lifecycle that starts with initiation and stakeholder alignment. At the start of a project, the team defines the business problem, success metrics, stakeholder list, and initial timeline. This helps validate whether the idea should move forward into planning. Once approved, work is organized into a clear backlog with priorities, milestones, responsibilities, and acceptance criteria to ensure the team is aligned on what needs to be delivered and when.

During execution, the organization emphasizes iterative delivery, regular communication, and proactive risk management. Teams hold daily standups, weekly syncs, and milestone reviews to track progress, surface blockers, and flag dependencies early. Core roles such as project managers, product managers, developers, QA, and stakeholders each contribute to delivery with clear responsibilities. Quality is built into the process through CI checks, testing, smoke validation, security scanning, and manual QA when needed.

The process also includes formal governance for risk, release, and continuous improvement. Risks are documented, monitored, and escalated through a clear path when they affect delivery or business outcomes. Release work includes staging and production verification, rollback planning, and stakeholder communication. After each sprint or major milestone, teams hold retrospectives to capture lessons learned and convert actions into backlog items for follow-up improvement.

## Purpose

These project management documents are intended to give teams a common framework for planning, delivery, coordination, and continuous improvement. They support a consistent operating model across projects and help reduce the risk of relying on informal or fragmented knowledge.

## How to Use These Docs

Use the overview and initiation guides when starting a new initiative. Move into planning and execution docs as the work becomes active, and revisit risk, release, and retrospective guidance throughout the project lifecycle. Together, these documents provide a practical, repeatable approach to cross-functional execution and project governance.
